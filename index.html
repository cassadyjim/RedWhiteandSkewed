About Red, White, & Skewed

Welcome to Red, White, and Skewed—where we take the day's most polarizing news story and show you how it's being spun by both sides of the political spectrum.

Our mission is simple: demonstrate how the same facts can be twisted into completely different narratives depending on your media diet. We present the most extreme conservative take, the most extreme liberal take, and then cut through the BS with a fact-checked analysis.

Why we do this: Because most Americans never see how "the other side" covers the news. We're living in separate information ecosystems, and that's dangerous for democracy. By putting these narratives side-by-side, we hope to:

  • Expose media manipulation techniques used across the political spectrum
  • Help readers identify bias in their own news sources
  • Encourage critical thinking about how stories are framed
  • Provide actual facts when both sides are playing fast and loose with reality

Important: The "Conservative Take" and "Liberal Take" articles are deliberately written to reflect the most extreme, partisan versions of each story—the kind you'd encounter in highly biased echo chambers. They are not endorsements of these views, but rather illustrations of how partisan media operates.

The Fact-Check section is where we do our actual journalism—calling out spin, verifying claims, and providing context that both sides conveniently omit.

Think critically. Read widely. Trust no one completely—including us. 🇺🇸

⚠️ Media Literacy Alert

This page is designed to be an educational tool. The partisan articles above use real rhetoric and framing techniques from actual media sources, but are intentionally exaggerated to make the bias obvious.

Red flags to watch for in ANY news source:

⚡ Emotional language designed to trigger anger or fear
⚡ Selective use of facts that support only one narrative
⚡ Demonization of the "other side" as evil rather than mistaken
⚡ Absence of counterarguments or competing perspectives
⚡ Anonymous sources making explosive claims
⚡ Headlines that don't match the actual content
⚡ Treating speculation or opinion as established fact

If your news source never criticizes "your side" and always portrays the opposition as villains, you're not getting news—you're getting propaganda.
