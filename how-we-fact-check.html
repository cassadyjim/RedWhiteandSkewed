How We Fact-Check

Our methodology for separating fact from spin

Our Approach

We examine how conservative and liberal media outlets cover the same story. We pull direct quotes from their reporting so you can see how each side frames the news.

When claims are made, we verify them against available evidence and note where outlets may be omitting context or spinning facts.

Our goal isn't to declare a "winner"—it's to help you see the difference between what happened and how it's being reported.

We use AI tools to help research and compile coverage from multiple sources. All content is reviewed for accuracy before publishing.

Common Spin Techniques We Flag

⚠️ Cherry-Picked Statistics

Choosing one data point that supports your narrative while ignoring contradicting data.

⚠️ Out-of-Context Quotes

Clipping quotes to change their meaning or omitting crucial qualifying statements.

⚠️ False Equivalence

Treating unequal things as equal to create a misleading "both sides" narrative.

⚠️ Strawman Arguments

Misrepresenting the opposing view to make it easier to attack.

⚠️ Loaded Language

Using emotionally charged words to influence perception rather than inform.

⚠️ Missing Context

Omitting crucial background information that would change interpretation.

⚠️ Unverified Claims

Stating assertions as fact without providing sources or evidence.

⚠️ Correlation vs. Causation

Implying one thing caused another when they're merely correlated.

✓ We Also Highlight Fair Points

Fact-checking isn't just about calling out falsehoods. When either side makes a valid point, cites accurate data, or raises a legitimate concern, we acknowledge it. Our goal is accuracy, not artificial balance or partisan attacks.

You'll see "Fair Point" callouts in our fact-checks when a partisan perspective gets something right—even when we're critical of their overall framing.

Our Limitations

We're not omniscient. We do our best, but we can make mistakes. We have our own blind spots. If you find an error, please tell us.

We can't check everything. Some stories involve classified information, developing situations, or claims that are genuinely impossible to verify. We're honest when we hit these limits.

We're not the final word. Our fact-checks are meant to be a starting point for your own research, not a replacement for it. We link to our sources so you can dig deeper.
