Frequently Asked Questions

What is Red, White, and Skewed?

We're a daily media literacy project that takes polarizing news stories and shows how they're being covered by partisan media on both sides. Each story features a conservative take, a liberal take, and a fact-check that calls out spin from both perspectives. Our goal is to expose how the same facts can be twisted into completely different narratives.

Are the "Conservative Take" and "Liberal Take" real articles?

They're composite representations of how partisan media is actually covering each story. We don't editorialize or create our own content—we simply report what's being reported, positioning contrasting views on the same story side by side. All quotes and claims are sourced from real outlets and commentators, and we link to every source so you can verify for yourself. The perspectives are presented to reflect how each side is actually framing the news.

Do you use AI to create the content?

Yes, and we're transparent about it. The research, collation, and formatting of each story is largely assisted by AI. This actually makes us well-suited for our mission: we're not injecting our own opinions or editorializing—we're simply gathering and presenting what partisan media is already reporting. AI is ideal for this because it can quickly survey coverage across the political spectrum without inherent bias. That said, AI can make errors, which is exactly why we source and link every claim. If something looks wrong, check the source and let us know.

How do you choose which stories to cover?

We look for stories that are generating significant partisan division—where conservative and liberal media are telling dramatically different versions of the same events. We prioritize national news with clear "spin potential" and avoid stories where there's broad consensus (those aren't as educational for our purposes).

Why do you show extreme versions of each side?

Because that's what many Americans are actually consuming. The most popular partisan media isn't moderate—it's inflammatory. By showing the most aggressive framing from each side, we make the manipulation techniques obvious. If we showed watered-down versions, readers might not recognize the same techniques in their own news consumption.

How do you fact-check your fact-checks?

We link to our sources so you can verify our claims. We prioritize primary sources (official documents, direct quotes, raw data) over secondary reporting. When we make a judgment call, we explain our reasoning. If we get something wrong, we correct it. See our How We Fact-Check page for our full methodology.

What does the poll measure?

The poll asks which perspective resonates with you after reading both sides. It's not a scientific survey—it's a way to see how our readers are responding to the story and to encourage engagement. We display aggregate results after you vote so you can see where other readers stand.

Can I suggest a story?

Yes! Email us at info@redwhiteandskewed.com with story suggestions. We especially appreciate tips on stories where you've noticed dramatically different coverage between outlets.

How can I support this project?

The best way to support us is to share our stories with people across the political spectrum. Challenge your friends to read both perspectives. Use our content to start conversations about media bias. If you're an educator, consider using our stories as teaching tools for media literacy.

Do you have a newsletter?

Not yet, but we're working on it. Check back soon or follow us on social media for updates.

I found an error. How do I report it?

Please email info@redwhiteandskewed.com with the story title, the error you found, and your source for the correction. We take accuracy seriously and will update stories when we get it wrong.
